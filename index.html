Latin Dance Chicago

A design evolution in 25 versions — exploring the best places to dance Latin rhythms in the city

by Enrique Zepeda

This gallery traces the journey of designing a website about Chicago's Latin dance scene. Beginning with ten distinct explorations, then combining the strongest ideas, and finally refining them into a polished result. Each version is a snapshot in that evolution — a frame on the wall of this design story.

Phase I

Exploration

Ten distinct approaches — varying in style, layout, tone, and audience. Casting a wide net to discover what resonates.

Version 1

Clean & Minimal

A stripped-down, list-based layout that lets the content speak for itself. Simple typography, generous whitespace, and zero distractions. Our starting point — what does the content look like with nothing in the way?

Version 2

Bold & Colorful

A vibrant, Latin-inspired color palette with energetic typography. Captures the warmth and excitement of the dance floor. What happens when we turn the energy all the way up?

Version 3

Dark / Nightlife

A moody, club-inspired aesthetic with dark backgrounds and glowing accents. Evokes the feeling of stepping into a salsa night. The dance scene lives after dark — should the site feel that way too?

Version 4

Beginner-Friendly

Approachable design with plenty of guidance text, welcoming language, and a FAQ section. What if the primary audience has never danced before?

Version 5

Map / Location-Focused

Organized by Chicago neighborhood with a color-coded legend. Instead of asking “what kind of dance?” this version asks “where are you?”

Version 6

Event-Calendar Style

A weekly schedule layout organized by day and time. Treats the content like a calendar — when can you dance, and where should you go?

Version 7

Magazine / Editorial

Editorial storytelling with a dropcap lede, pull quotes, and longform descriptions. What if the site read like a feature article in a magazine?

Version 8

Social-Media Inspired

An Instagram-like feed with story bubbles, gradient tiles, usernames, and engagement counts. What if discovering dance venues felt like scrolling your feed?

Version 9

Community / Forum

Reddit-style upvote threads, dancer testimonials, and featured profiles. What if the site was built around the people and their voices, not just the venues?

Version 10

Mobile-First

Designed for phone screens with a sticky tab bar, bottom navigation, “tonight” banner, and compact cards. What if the user is checking this on their phone before heading out?

Phase II

Combinations

After reviewing Phase I, dark themes won out. Five versions explore different dark aesthetics — neon, gold, tropical, minimalist, carnival. Five more tell distinct user stories, each guiding a different persona from curiosity to action.

Version 11

Neon Elegance

Coral, violet, and blue neon accents on deep black. Lessons grouped by dance type, day-by-day socials calendar, sticky nav pills, and a glowing “Tonight’s the Night” CTA. The nightlife aesthetic meets structured information.

Version 12

Golden Hour

Warm gold, red, and teal on dark. Lessons grouped by school with full cards, icon-based navigation, stats bar, and a “Join the Scene” banner. The feeling of a fun, elegant night out.

Version 13

Midnight Tropical

Hot pink and tropical green on deep teal-black. Lessons grouped by neighborhood, a floating discovery bar, card-per-day socials, and a lush “The Music is Playing Somewhere” CTA. Latin club meets botanical garden.

Version 14

Minimalist Noir

Pure black with a single champagne gold accent. Ultra-refined typography, minimalist list layouts, and maximum restraint. What if a luxury cocktail bar designed a dance guide?

Version 15

Electric Carnival

Four vibrant colors — orange, cyan, red, lime — on dark. Maximum energy with bold Poppins 900 headings, color-coded dance types, and a party-flyer aesthetic. “NEVER DANCED BEFORE? PERFECT.”

Version 16

The Curious Beginner

User story: Alex, 28, never danced before. “What to Expect” FAQ, lessons grouped by experience level, “Free Lesson Included” badges on socials. Every element says: you belong here.

Version 17

Tonight’s Dancer

User story: Maria, 32, visiting Chicago and wants to dance tonight. Compact hero, tonight’s event as the primary content, day-of-week nav strip. Speed is everything — find your floor in 5 seconds.

Version 18

New to Chicago

User story: David, 35, just moved from Miami. Large neighborhood map with scene descriptions, studios with “Community Vibe” notes, and “regulars” flavor text. Finding a new dance home.

Version 19

Date Night

User story: Sarah & Tom, couple seeking a unique date. A 3-step timeline, date-night star ratings, dinner-and-dance neighborhood combos. “The couple that dances together stays together.”

Version 20

The Comeback Dancer

User story: Carlos, 45, hasn’t danced in 15 years. “What’s Changed, What Hasn’t” two-column bridge, lessons grouped by comeback readiness, and nostalgic “The Floor Remembers You” messaging.

Phase III

Refinements

The final five versions polish the strongest combinations — refining typography, spacing, accessibility, and adding finishing touches.

Version 21

The Definitive Guide

The culmination of 20 iterations. v14’s minimalist typography and restraint + v12’s warm gold palette + v17’s tonight-first urgency + v16’s beginner onboarding. A site that answers “what’s on tonight?” for regulars while saying “you belong here” to newcomers.
