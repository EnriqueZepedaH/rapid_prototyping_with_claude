A Guide to Chicago's Latin Dance Scene

Where the City Moves to Latin Rhythms

From Pilsen street corners to River North dance floors, Chicago pulses with salsa, bachata, and cumbia every night of the week.

Find Your First Class
Spring 2026 Chicago, Illinois The Dance Issue

Chicago has always been a city of movement. Its Latin dance scene is one of the most vibrant in the Midwest — a living, breathing network of studios, clubs, and community spaces where the rhythms of salsa, bachata, cumbia, and merengue bring people together every single week. Whether you're taking your first step or your ten-thousandth, there's a place for you here.

Chicago's dance studios come alive every evening, filling with music and movement.

The Classroom

Where Chicago learns to dance

An evening class at Latin Street Music & Dancing in the South Loop

Latin Street Music & Dancing

600 W Roosevelt Rd, South Loop • Mon–Fri 6–10pm, Sat 12–5pm

For more than twenty-seven years, this South Loop institution has been the first stop for anyone serious about learning to move. With over eighty weekly classes split across salsa, bachata, and cumbia, and a rotating cast of ten-plus instructors, Latin Street runs like a well-oiled machine — walk in on a Monday evening and every studio is packed, the music bleeding through the walls. Free parking sweetens the deal, but it's the sheer depth of the schedule that keeps dancers coming back.

Dance Academy of Salsa

2246 N Western Ave, Logan Square • Mon–Fri 5:30–10pm, Sat 1:30–5pm

This is the studio that shaped a generation of Chicago salseros. Family-owned since 2002, Dance Academy of Salsa runs four-week progressive courses in salsa, bachata, and merengue for $110 — or you can drop in to any class and pay at the door. The emphasis is on musicality, not just moves. By the time you finish a cycle, you can hear the break in the music before your feet even think about it.

Dennis PaSamba Dance Company

4820 W Irving Park Rd, Six Corners • Evenings

Dennis PaSamba has been teaching Chicago to dance for over thirty-three years, and his Six Corners studio remains one of the city's best-kept secrets. The repertoire spans salsa, bachata, cumbia, and kizomba — a rare breadth under one roof. Four-week sessions run $95, drop-ins are $15, and free parking makes the trek to Irving Park painless. It's as much a cultural event as a dance class.

Latin Rhythms Academy

West Loop • Mon–Fri evenings, Sat mornings

Latin Rhythms Academy takes a curriculum-based approach to salsa, mambo, bachata, and kizomba that appeals to the detail-oriented dancer. Four-week sessions cost $90 and build systematically — footwork one week, partner connection the next — so progress feels tangible. The West Loop location draws an after-work crowd that fills weeknight classes, while Saturday mornings bring a mellower energy perfect for drilling technique.

Chicago International Salsa Congress

Westin O'Hare, Rosemont • Annual every February

Once a year, the Westin O'Hare in Rosemont transforms into the epicenter of salsa in the Midwest. The Chicago International Salsa Congress packs more than sixty workshops into a single weekend, led by world-class instructors from across the globe. Evening showcases and social dancing run until dawn. It's the event that reminds you just how deep this city's dance community goes.

The Dance Floor

Where Chicago dances after dark

The energy of a Salsa Sunday night at The Cubby Bear in Wrigleyville

Salsa Sundays at The Cubby Bear

1059 W Addison St, Wrigleyville • Sundays 7pm–1am • $15 cover

This is the one. DJ Marz commands the decks while the Wrigleyville landmark fills wall-to-wall with dancers who know exactly why they're here. A free lesson at 8:15 pm — taught by the crew from Latin Rhythms Academy — gives newcomers just enough courage to hit the floor. By 10 o'clock the room is in full swing, couples weaving through the crowd in effortless spins. If you dance salsa in Chicago, you end up at The Cubby Bear eventually.

Salsa & Bachata at Alhambra Palace

1240 W Randolph St, West Loop • Thursdays 8pm–1am • $10/$15

Alhambra Palace is opulent in the best way — ornate Moorish arches, warm lighting, and a floor full of dancers who came specifically for the Thursday night ritual. DJs Matador and Jesus Castro trade sets, mixing salsa and bachata all night long. The lesson starts at 8, the social kicks off at 9, and by midnight the energy in the West Loop venue is electric.

Havana Grill Latin Nights

412 N Clark St, River North • Fri & Sat, music at 11:30pm • No cover

Step inside this 1940s art-deco jewel in River North and the night unfolds on its own schedule. Dinner comes first — Cuban classics done right — and then the live Latin bands take over well past midnight. Friday and Saturday nights, the dance floor materializes between the tables, and nobody seems to mind that there's no cover charge. Havana Grill is the kind of place where the food and the dancing are equally worth the trip.

Dennis PaSamba Friday Social

4820 W Irving Park Rd, Six Corners • Fridays, free lesson 7pm, social until 10:30pm • $10

Dennis PaSamba doesn't just teach — he throws a party. Every Friday evening, the Six Corners studio opens its doors for a free lesson at 7 followed by open social dancing until 10:30. Ten dollars gets you in. The crowd is a mix of PaSamba students and visitors from across the city, and the vibe never takes itself too seriously.

Mayambo Monthly Social

Location varies (mayambo.com) • Monthly

Mayambo keeps its community guessing — each month, the salsa-focused social pops up at a different venue around the city. Check mayambo.com for the latest location and details. What stays constant is the crowd: a tight-knit circle of salseros who show up for the music, the dancing, and each other.

The energy never stops — Chicago's Latin dance scene runs seven nights a week.
